# Ehlers cycle and filter operators: domain review pack (F3)

This pack is for the user's domain review of the Ehlers operators before 3.0.0.
For each operator it gives the published formula and its source, a link to our
code, the known-signal checks with plots, and the result. The automated checks
live in [`tests/test_ehlers_known_signals.py`](../../../tests/test_ehlers_known_signals.py)
(48 cases, about 1 s). A check that fails because of the operator is marked
`xfail(strict=True)` there with the same reason as below, so the suite stays
green and the failure stays visible. Nothing in the operators was changed.

The plots are regenerated by
[`ehlers/make_plots.py`](ehlers/make_plots.py), which imports the signals from the
test file. On every plot the expected value is a black dashed line and the
tolerance is a grey band.

Scope: the tranche commit `ed24353` brought 18 operators. Twelve are Ehlers
operators and are reviewed here. The other six (PlusDM, MinusDM, PlusDI,
MinusDI, DX, ADXR) are Wilder's directional movement family, not Ehlers, and are
left out. The brief's list maps onto the real operators as follows:

- "DominantCycle, CycleFrequency, HilbertPhasor, CyclePhase, CycleAmplitude,
  CycleSine, TrendMode, MAMA, InstantaneousTrendline": all exist under these names.
- "super smoother, roofing": `SuperSmoother`, `RoofingFilter`. The tranche also
  has `Decycler`, which the brief did not name; it is reviewed here.
- "high-pass": **no such operator.** Ehlers' 2-pole high-pass exists only as the
  first stage inside `RoofingFilter`, and his 1-pole high-pass only as the
  complement inside `Decycler`. `ButterHighpass` is a Butterworth design, not Ehlers.
- `HilbertCycle` (named in the merge message) is the shared internal engine in
  [`detail/hilbert_cycle.h`](../../../include/screamer/detail/hilbert_cycle.h), not
  a public operator. Nine of the twelve operators sit on it.

## Sources and tolerances

Books, with what could be checked from here (the books themselves were not
available):

- Ehlers, *Rocket Science for Traders* (Wiley, 2001, ISBN 978-0-471-40567-2).
  Chapter titles "Hilbert Transforms", "Measuring Cycle Periods", "The Sinewave
  Indicator", "The Instantaneous Trendline", "Identifying Market Modes" and
  "MAMA, The Mother of Adaptive Moving Averages" are confirmed from Wiley's table
  of contents as quoted by a web search (the Wiley page itself did not load). The
  sinewave indicator as chapter 9 rests on a TradingView write-up and the table
  order. Other chapter numbers and all page or figure numbers: **not verified**.
  The book gives formulas as EasyLanguage listings rather than numbered
  equations; no equation numbers are cited for that reason.
- Ehlers, *Cybernetic Analysis for Stocks and Futures* (Wiley, 2004). The 2-pole
  Instantaneous Trendline with a fixed `alpha` (about 0.07) in chapter 3 comes
  from secondary sources (a TradingView script, LuxAlgo). **Not verified.**
- Ehlers, *Cycle Analytics for Traders* (Wiley, 2013). Chapter 3 smoothing
  filters (SuperSmoother), chapter 4 decyclers, chapter 7 the roofing filter,
  from a library catalog record seen through a web search. **Not verified
  against the book.**
- Ehlers, "Predictive and successful indicators", *S&C* 32(1), 16-25, January
  2014: the roofing filter. Verified in F1.
- Ehlers, "MESA Adaptive Moving Averages", *S&C*, September 2001: the usual
  citation for MAMA. **Not verified.**

Independent reference: TA-Lib 0.7.1 (in the dev venv) implements the Rocket
Science routines as `HT_DCPERIOD`, `HT_DCPHASE`, `HT_PHASOR`, `HT_SINE`,
`HT_TRENDLINE`, `HT_TRENDMODE` and `MAMA`. Where we share its front end the
numbers agree to 1e-5, so it serves as a stand-in for the published code.

Tolerances: no published accuracy figure for any of these operators could be
verified, so the tolerances are those of the brief (5% for period and amplitude
on a sine, 10% with at most one period of lag on a chirp, 1 dB around -3 dB at a
filter cutoff). The -3 dB criterion matches the usual definition of a cutoff;
that Ehlers defines his cutoff periods as the half-power point is **not
verified**. Checks added beyond the brief use 5% for rates and ratios, 5 degrees
for phase wander, one sample for leads, and 10% of the amplitude for a trend
estimate; these are our choices.

Signals: a unit sine of period P in {10, 20, 40} over 1200 samples; a chirp
whose period moves linearly from 15 to 40 (and back) over 1500 samples; the
same sine plus a ramp that rises one amplitude per cycle (slope 1/P). The
evaluation starts at sample 200. The engine reports from sample 41, but its
period loop starts at zero, may grow only 1.5x per step, and is smoothed twice,
so the first 100 or so samples still carry the start-up transient. The dotted
vertical line on the plots marks sample 200.

## The shared Hilbert engine

Code: [`include/screamer/detail/hilbert_cycle.h`](../../../include/screamer/detail/hilbert_cycle.h).
Published formula (Rocket Science, "Measuring Cycle Periods", the homodyne
discriminator; reproduced in the header comment and in TA-Lib), angles in degrees:

```
Smooth    = (4 P + 3 P[1] + 2 P[2] + P[3]) / 10
Detrender = (.0962 Smooth + .5769 Smooth[2] - .5769 Smooth[4] - .0962 Smooth[6]) (.075 Period[1] + .54)
Q1 = same FIR on Detrender,  I1 = Detrender[3]
jI = same FIR on I1,         jQ = same FIR on Q1
I2 = I1 - jQ,  Q2 = Q1 + jI,  then I2, Q2 smoothed .2 / .8
Re = I2 I2[1] + Q2 Q2[1],  Im = I2 Q2[1] - Q2 I2[1],  then smoothed .2 / .8
Period = 360 / arctan(Im / Re), limited to [.67, 1.5] x Period[1] and [6, 50],
         smoothed .2 / .8;  SmoothPeriod = .33 Period + .67 SmoothPeriod[1]
```

Our code follows this line for line. Two points the review should know:

1. The period is clamped to [6, 50] samples, as published. The DominantCycle page
   does not say so; a 60-sample cycle reads 50.
2. The engine exposes two different phasors. `CyclePhase` (and so `CycleSine`,
   `TrendMode`, `MAMA`) uses `atan2(Q1, I1)`. `HilbertPhasor` and
   `CycleAmplitude` use the smoothed homodyne pair `(I2, Q2)`. The pages say all
   of them "share the same in-phase and quadrature components", which is not
   accurate.

## DominantCycle

Formula: SmoothPeriod above. Code:
[`dominant_cycle.h`](../../../include/screamer/dominant_cycle.h).

| Check | Expected | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sine, P 10 / 20 / 40, max relative error | at most 5% | 1.0% / 0.2% / 0.4% | PASS |
| Chirp 15 to 40, error against P(t - L), L at most one period | at most 10% | 0.3% (up), 0.7% (down) | PASS |
| Sine plus trend, after `RoofingFilter(48, 8)` | at most 5% | 1.0% / 0.2% / 0.4% | PASS |
| Random walk against TA-Lib `HT_DCPERIOD` | at most 1e-4 | 8e-6 | PASS |

![DominantCycle on sines](ehlers/dominant_cycle_sine.png)

![DominantCycle on a chirp](ehlers/dominant_cycle_chirp.png)

On the chirp the estimate trails the true period by 16 to 20 samples, about
half a cycle, and stays within 2.5% even without allowing for lag.

![DominantCycle with a trend](ehlers/dominant_cycle_trend.png)

On the raw sine-plus-ramp the estimate is biased: medians 12.3, 20.3, 40.2 for
P 10, 20, 40. TA-Lib gives the same numbers to 1e-11, so this is a property of
the published discriminator, not of our port. Ehlers measures cycles on
roofing-filtered data, and the check follows that recipe. Suggestion for the
docs (not done): say on the DominantCycle page that a trending input should go
through `RoofingFilter` first, and mention the [6, 50] clamp.

## CycleAmplitude

Formula: `sqrt(I2^2 + Q2^2)`, our own read of the homodyne internals. Ehlers
uses `I2, Q2` only to form `Re, Im` for the period; no published amplitude
estimator on them was found. Code:
[`cycle_amplitude.h`](../../../include/screamer/cycle_amplitude.h).

| Check | Expected | Measured (median) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit sine, P 10 / 20 / 40 | 1 within 5% | 0.41 / 1.08 / 1.41 | **FAIL** |

![CycleAmplitude on sines](ehlers/cycle_amplitude_sine.png)

Cause: `I2 = I1 - jQ` adds two near-equal phasors (about twice the input), and
the .2/.8 smoothing then shrinks a rotating phasor by a period-dependent factor,
strongly at short periods. The output scales with the input amplitude but its
gain depends on the period, so it is not the envelope the page describes. For
comparison TA-Lib `HT_PHASOR` returns `(I1, Q1)`, whose magnitude reads 0.96 /
1.02 / 0.91. A fix is a design choice for the user: read the magnitude of
`(I1, Q1)` (closer, still about 9% low at P 40), or normalise by the known gain.

## HilbertPhasor

Formula: outputs `(I2, Q2)` of the engine. Code:
[`hilbert_phasor.h`](../../../include/screamer/hilbert_phasor.h).

| Check | Expected | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| RMS(Q) / RMS(I), P 10 / 20 / 40 | 1 within 5% | 0.98 / 1.00 / 1.00 | PASS |
| Lead of Q over I | P/4 within 1 sample | 3 / 5 / 10 samples | PASS |

![HilbertPhasor](ehlers/hilbert_phasor.png)

Q leads I. This is Ehlers' convention: the 7-tap Hilbert FIR weights the newest
sample positively, so it acts as a differentiator, and I1 is the delayed centre
sample. It is the conjugate of the textbook analytic signal (where Q lags I).
The page calls Q "a version of the input phase-shifted by 90 degrees" without a
sign, which is fine, but the user may want the direction stated. The magnitude
is CycleAmplitude, so its scaling note applies here too.

## CyclePhase

Formula: `atan2(Q1, I1)` in degrees, wrapped to [0, 360). Ehlers' published
phase is `arctan(Q1 / I1)` (range -90 to 90); the sinewave indicator in Rocket
Science uses a different phase again, the DC phase from a DFT over one dominant
cycle (TA-Lib `HT_DCPHASE`). Code: [`cycle_phase.h`](../../../include/screamer/cycle_phase.h).

| Check | Expected | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase change per sample, P 10 / 20 / 40 | -360/P within 5% | 0.97 / 1.00 / 1.00 of it | PASS |
| Offset to the input phase, std after settling | at most 5 degrees | 2.3 / 0.9 / 1.2 | PASS |

![CyclePhase](ehlers/cycle_phase.png)

The phase **decreases** by 360/P per sample. This is consistent with Ehlers,
whose MAMA takes `DeltaPhase = Phase[1] - Phase` as positive on a cycle. The
page says the phase "advances by roughly 360 / period degrees per sample",
which reads as increasing; a doc fix. The offset to the input is a fixed filter
delay that depends on P (about -110, 126, 63 degrees for P 10, 20, 40).

## CycleSine

Formula as published (Rocket Science, "The Sinewave Indicator"): `Sine =
sin(DCPhase)`, `LeadSine = sin(DCPhase + 45)`, where DCPhase increases with time.
Ours uses `CyclePhase`, which decreases. Code:
[`cycle_sine.h`](../../../include/screamer/cycle_sine.h).

| Check | Expected | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Period of `sine` from zero crossings | P within 5% | within 1% | PASS |
| Lead of `leadsine` over `sine` | +P/8 within 1 sample (1.25 / 2.5 / 5) | -1 / -2 / -5 | **FAIL** |

![CycleSine against TA-Lib HT_SINE](ehlers/cycle_sine.png)

Adding 45 degrees to a phase that runs backwards moves the curve back in time,
so `leadsine` lags `sine` by one eighth of a cycle. That defeats its purpose
(the crossing is meant to anticipate the turn). TA-Lib's `HT_SINE` on the same
input leads by P/8 (bottom panel). Possible fixes: use `sin(-phase)` and
`sin(-phase + 45)`, or `sin(phase - 45)` for the lead, or adopt Ehlers' DC
phase. The user should pick; the existing test in `tests/test_cycle.py` only
checks bounds, which is why this went unnoticed.

## TrendMode

Formula: our own rule, as the page states. Trend (1) when the per-sample phase
change is below `phase_rate_frac * 360 / period`, else cycle (0). Ehlers'
published trend mode (Rocket Science, "Identifying Market Modes"; TA-Lib
`HT_TRENDMODE`) uses several conditions, including price against the trendline.
Code: [`trend_mode.h`](../../../include/screamer/trend_mode.h).

| Check | Expected | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sine plus trend (slope 1/P), share of samples flagged cycle | at least 90% | 100% for P 10, 20, 40 | PASS |
| Pure ramp, share flagged trend | at least 90% | 64% | **FAIL** |

![TrendMode](ehlers/trend_mode.png)

The brief asks that "the reported trend matches the trend". TrendMode outputs
only a flag, so that part is checked on InstantaneousTrendline below.

On a pure ramp the detrended signal is nearly constant, and the engine's
period/phase loop then oscillates by itself (the period feeds back into the FIR
gain `.075 Period + .54`). The phase jumps back and forth, so about a third of
the samples read as cycling. TA-Lib flags 100% of the same ramp as trend. Note
that with a steeper ramp (two amplitudes per cycle at P 40) our TrendMode flags
13% trend, so the rule responds to trend only weakly. This needs a design
decision: tune the rule, add a price-versus-trendline condition as Ehlers does,
or document the limitation.

## InstantaneousTrendline

Formula, as in our code: the 2-pole recursion

```
it = (a - a^2/4) x + a^2/2 x[1] - (a - 3a^2/4) x[2] + 2 (1-a) it[1] - (1-a)^2 it[2]
```

with `a = 2 / (SmoothPeriod + 1)`. This recursion is the one attributed to
*Cybernetic Analysis* chapter 3, where `a` is a fixed constant around 0.07
(**not verified**). The Rocket Science version ("The Instantaneous Trendline";
TA-Lib `HT_TRENDLINE`) is different: a simple average of price over one
dominant cycle, then a 4-tap WMA. Ours combines the two (the Cybernetic filter,
the Rocket Science adaptive period); the page cites only Cybernetic Analysis.
Code: [`instantaneous_trendline.h`](../../../include/screamer/instantaneous_trendline.h).

| Check | Expected | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pure ramp, max error | at most 1e-6 | 2e-11 | PASS |
| Sine plus trend, max error to the trend | at most 0.1 of the amplitude | 0.54 / 0.68 / 0.84 | **FAIL** |

![InstantaneousTrendline](ehlers/instantaneous_trendline.png)

The recursion is the complement of Ehlers' 2-pole high-pass, so it passes a
ramp with no lag, as published. But with `a = 2 / (P + 1)` its gain at the
dominant period is about 0.57 to 0.58 for every P (computed and measured agree),
so more than half of the cycle stays in the output. The page says the trendline
"removes the dominant cycle from the output", which it does not. TA-Lib's
`HT_TRENDLINE` removes the cycle completely but lags the ramp (red line). A fix
is a design choice: the Rocket Science average over one cycle, or a smaller
`a` (Ehlers' 0.07 passes 22% / 44% / 78% for P 10 / 20 / 40, so a fixed `a`
does not remove the cycle either).

## MAMA

Formula (docs page, and TA-Lib): `DeltaPhase = max(Phase[1] - Phase, 1)`,
`alpha = clip(fast_limit / DeltaPhase, slow_limit, fast_limit)`, MAMA an EMA with
`alpha`, FAMA an EMA of MAMA with `alpha / 2`. Ehlers' phase is
`arctan(Q1 / I1)`; ours is `CyclePhase`, `atan2(Q1, I1)`. Code:
[`mama.h`](../../../include/screamer/mama.h).

| Check | Expected | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Effective alpha between wraps, P 10 / 20 / 40 | `clip(0.5 P / 360, 0.05, 0.5)` = 0.05 / 0.05 / 0.056 within 5% | 0.05 / 0.05 / 0.056 | PASS |
| `fast_limit` steps per cycle | 1 within 5% (our phase) | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.02 | PASS |

![MAMA alpha](ehlers/mama.png)

Two notes for the user:

1. With Ehlers' `arctan` the phase wraps every half cycle, so `alpha` jumps to
   `fast_limit` twice per cycle; TA-Lib shows 2.0 per cycle on the same sines.
   Ours wraps once. The page already says TA-Lib differs "in front end"; this is
   the concrete difference, and it changes how often MAMA snaps to price. Keep
   or align is a design choice.
2. The page and the parameter descriptions describe the adaptation backwards.
   They say `alpha` rises toward `fast_limit` "when the phase advances quickly".
   By the formula a fast phase advance gives a large `DeltaPhase` and a small
   `alpha` (slow); `alpha` reaches `fast_limit` when the phase stalls or wraps.
   A doc fix.

## SuperSmoother

Formula (Cycle Analytics, chapter 3 per the catalog; not verified):
`a1 = exp(-1.414 pi / P)`, `b1 = 2 a1 cos(1.414 pi / P)`, `c2 = b1`,
`c3 = -a1^2`, `c1 = 1 - c2 - c3`, `y = c1 (x + x[1]) / 2 + c2 y[1] + c3 y[2]`.
Code: [`super_smoother.h`](../../../include/screamer/super_smoother.h),
coefficients in [`signal/ehlers.h`](../../../include/screamer/signal/ehlers.h).
The existing `tests/test_ehlers_filters.py` matches this formula to 1e-12.

Gain at the cutoff period, P 10 / 20 / 40: -3.16 / -3.05 / -3.02 dB, tolerance
-3 dB within 1 dB. **PASS.** The true -3 dB point sits at 10.17 / 20.08 / 40.05.

## Decycler

Formula (Cycle Analytics, chapter 4 per the catalog; not verified):
`alpha = (cos(360/P) + sin(360/P) - 1) / cos(360/P)`,
`y = alpha/2 (x + x[1]) + (1 - alpha) y[1]`. Code:
[`decycler.h`](../../../include/screamer/decycler.h).

Gain at the cutoff, P 20 / 60: -3.01 / -3.01 dB. **PASS.** At the cutoff the
Decycler passes 71% of a cycle, so "cycles at or below this length are removed"
on the page is loose; only cycles well below the cutoff are removed. On a ramp
it lags by about 9.5 samples at P 60 (informational).

## RoofingFilter

Formula (S&C January 2014, verified in F1 as the source; the formula itself not
checked against the article): a 2-pole high-pass with
`alpha = (cos(.707 360/HP) + sin(.707 360/HP) - 1) / cos(.707 360/HP)`,
`hp = (1 - alpha/2)^2 (x - 2x[1] + x[2]) + 2(1 - alpha) hp[1] - (1 - alpha)^2 hp[2]`,
then a SuperSmoother at the low-pass period. Code:
[`roofing_filter.h`](../../../include/screamer/roofing_filter.h).

Gain with `hp_period=48, lp_period=10`: -3.54 dB at 48, -3.33 dB at 10.
**PASS.** Each edge carries a little extra loss from the other stage.

![Filter gains](ehlers/filters_gain.png)

## Decisions for the user

1. CycleSine `leadsine` lags: pick the fix (negate the phase, subtract 45, or
   adopt the DC phase).
2. CycleAmplitude and HilbertPhasor scaling: normalise, switch to `(I1, Q1)`, or
   document the amplitude as relative only.
3. TrendMode on a pure ramp: tune, extend toward Ehlers' rule, or document.
4. InstantaneousTrendline keeps more than half of the cycle: change the
   filter, or rewrite the page's claim.
5. MAMA wraps once per cycle where Ehlers' arctan wraps twice: keep or align.
6. Doc fixes without a design choice: phase direction (CyclePhase), MAMA
   adaptation wording, the [6, 50] clamp and roofing advice (DominantCycle),
   "shares the same I and Q" (CyclePhase, CycleAmplitude), Decycler cutoff
   wording, InstantaneousTrendline citation.
